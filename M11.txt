
//semantics 
  this is about how words carry meanings and how computers understand word meaning 

//lemma and wordforms 
 lemma - base form of a word 
  same stem + same parts of speech 
  ex: run 
 wordform 
  actual form that appears in text 
  ex: banks, ran 

Lemma = dictionary form
Wordform = inflected form appearing in text

one lemma can have multiple senses(meanings)
 ex: bank 
 this introduces word sense ambiguity 

Homonymy 
 same form,unrelated meanings 
  ex: bank ,bat

Homographs: same spelling (bank/bank),bat
Homophones: same sound (“write” vs “right”)

because these might cause issues during 
 information retrieval 
   ex: bat care 
 machine translation 
   ex: bat 
 text-to speech 
  ex: bass 

polysemy 
  one word with multiple related meanings 
   ex: key, bank 

metonymy or systematic polysemy 
   Some words regularly shift meaning between related types.

synonyms 
 words with same meaning in some contexts 
  ex: couch/sofa , vomit/throwup 
 Two lexemes are synonyms if they can be substituted everywhere

 perfect synonyms cannot exist 

antonyms 
 senses that are opposites with respect to one feature of meaning 
  ex: dark/light 

hyponym & hypernym 
 hyponym - subclass 
 hypernym - superclass 

hyponyms and instances 
 wordnet has both classes and instnaces 
  Classes (city, vehicle)
  Instances (San Francisco, BMW)

applications :
 information extraction 
 information retrieval 
 question answering 
 machine translation 

 wordnet 3.0 - hierarchial lexical database 

//word-similarity 
 similarity != synonyms 
 Similarity depends on shared features.
  ex: bank 
  Similarity is calculated between senses, not just words.

//applications 
 used in :
  QA, machine translation , document clustering 

//word similarity vs relatedness 
  Similar words = near synonyms
 Related words = may not be similar

 car – bicycle (similar)
 car – gasoline (related but not similar)

 //two classes of similarity algos 
  1. thesaurus-based (wordnet)
  2. distributional (context similarity)
